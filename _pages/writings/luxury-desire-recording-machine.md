Luxury smartwatch copy reads uncannily like ankle-monitor copy. <i>Luxury Desire Recording Machine</i> is a text-generation experiment that fuses product blurbs for CCTV cameras, AI dashcams, smartwatches, and ankle monitors into one composite mega-product, paced by the repeated refrain, “I want.”

In “Luxury Surveillance,” Chris Gilliard and David Golumbia show how the same technologies split into two regimes: a paid, aspirational tier for the powerful and a compulsory, punitive tier for the marginalized. Smartwatches, wellness trackers, airport fast lanes, and smart doorbells sell convenience and self-care while optimizing data extraction and prediction. Their counterparts, such as court-ordered ankle monitors, e-carceration wearables and migrant tracking, apply the same logics with different packaging. Together, they expand surveillance capitalism and normalize unequal exposure to monitoring. <i>Luxury Desire Recording Machine</i> collapses these regimes, layering desire, privilege, status, and control within a single text.

The poem emerges from two computational steps. First, ChatGPT gathered concise, present-tense product descriptions of the devices. Then a simple Python script randomly recombined those verb-action phrases into a continuous narrative.

**References:** [Luxury Surveillance](https://reallifemag.com/luxury-surveillance/) by Chris Gilliard and David Golumbia, 2021.
